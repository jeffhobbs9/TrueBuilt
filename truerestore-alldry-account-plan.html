The +11.6% isn't magic: a fully documented job gives the adjuster no reason to deny line items. Complete proof → fewer denials and supplements → more of the scope you already performed gets approved. Carriers approve what's proven; TrueRestore makes every line provable.

How I'd actually land them — the two moves that de-risk it

1 · Coexist, don't rip-and-replace

The whole network motion hinges on getting owners off Encircle — and "white-glove migration" is a slogan unless I make switching risk-free. So I don't ask them to drop Encircle. I have them keep it and run TrueRestore on 3–4 live claims in parallel — a short, comped trial, so they're never double-paying. The owner feels the higher approved ticket on their own jobs with zero downside, then drops Encircle at switch — which is where the software-wash economics in the ROI tab kick in. It's a trial, not a migration — that turns the single biggest objection (switching cost) into a no-brainer, and produces the before/after proof I need for the case study anyway.

2 · Beachhead = multi-unit owners

I wouldn't spread the first wave thin across metros. I'd concentrate it on the multi-unit owners who run 4+ territories. One sale there = several locations converted and a natural, respected Franchise Advisory Council voice — density and credibility in a single relationship. In a peer network, density beats breadth: a few undeniable proof clusters tip the network faster than scattered logos. Land the loud, respected multi-unit owners and the middle of the S-curve follows.

The contrarian call — where I'd zag

I would not chase the franchisor's endorsement first — and I'd defend that. The obvious move is to run top-down for a preferred-vendor blessing on day one. But endorsement without proof is weak, and a lukewarm "we evaluated it" from the franchisor can actively inoculate the network against us — now every owner has a reason to wait. So I'd deliberately stay bottoms-up until 8–10 vocal, results-backed owners exist, then walk into the franchisor with a groundswell they can't ignore — so endorsement is a formality, not a favor.

Defense if pushed ("but the franchisor could accelerate everything"): only if they're genuinely enthusiastic — and enthusiasm is bought with owner proof, not slides. I sequence to earn a strong yes, not extract a weak one. Earn the FAC; don't beg it.

What "qualified" and "committed" really mean here

Qualified — the franchise nuance

  • Fit: insurance/claims-driven, meaningful monthly job volume, currently on Encircle or manual double-entry.
  • Pain: owner feels cycle time, double work, or tickets left on the table — in their own words.
  • Authority: the owner is the buyer (no franchisor sign-off needed) — but check whether they defer to the FAC or a multi-unit boss.
  • Timing: a real trigger — busy season, an Encircle renewal, a bad-cycle-time month.

Committed — the anti-drift test

  • A date, not a vibe: a scheduled go-live, not "sounds good, circle back."
  • Migration owned: a concrete plan to move off Encircle — data, habit, who trains the crew.
  • A named user: the person on the truck who'll actually run it, bought in.
  • Success defined: owner has agreed what "this worked" looks like in 30 days (so renewal is pre-sold).

Where this
could stall — and my answer.

Closing a network of independent owners is not a top-down sale, and pretending otherwise is how these deals die. Here's how I actually think a franchise network adopts, where AllDry specifically could stall, and what I'd do about each.

Risk · No one can force it

Franchisors rarely mandate a revenue tool onto independent owners; owners resist mandates on principle. So there's no single signature that wins 175.

Answer: Don't chase a mandate. Chase endorsement + peer-proof + owner economics. The FAC and preferred-vendor status do the persuading a mandate can't.

Source: IFA / FAC adoption literature — franchisees "reluctant to accept mandates."

Risk · Quiet users churn silently

San Bernardino and Austin are already transactional. A quiet user who never adopts becomes negative proof — "we had it, didn't stick."

Answer: Treat adoption as part of the sale (see pipeline). Fix these two before scaling; they're the canary. If I can't convert a quiet user to advocate, I don't yet have a network motion.

Risk · Encircle switching cost

Data, workflow habit, and crew muscle-memory live on Encircle. "Sounds great, but not now" is the default outcome.

Answer: White-glove migration as a product, not a promise — data handling, side-by-side on one live job, and a trained named user before go-live. Own the switch or don't count the deal.

Risk · Franchisor lukewarm / distracted

If the franchisor sees this as "a vendor's problem," I lose the endorsement lever and the conference stage.

Answer: Frame it as their KPI — franchisee profitability and consistency — with Seattle's numbers. Make Schlafer the internal hero, not me.

Risk · Owner economics are heterogeneous

178 independent P&Ls; a low-volume shop may not feel +11.6% the way a busy metro does. One ROI story won't fit all.

Answer: Segment. Lead with high-claims-volume territories where the math is loudest; use a per-owner ROI model (the ROI tab) on their real numbers instead of a network average.

Risk · The +11.6% is one data point

A single case study is fragile in front of a skeptical FAC. If it doesn't generalize, credibility craters.

Answer: Broaden the evidence before the FAC — San Bernardino/Austin + a couple of first-wave wins — so the claim rests on a pattern, not an anecdote. Under-promise the number, over-deliver on the proof.

My honest read on the adoption curve

A franchise network doesn't adopt on a straight line — it adopts on an S-curve gated by trust, over 2–3 years, not one. The first ~10–15% (Seattle + first-wave influencers) is the hardest and slowest because it's pure proof-building. Once the FAC endorses and a few respected owners are vocal, the middle of the curve moves fast on peer-proof and busy-season urgency. The tail (low-volume, change-averse holdouts) may never fully close, and that's fine — network standard doesn't require 100%; it requires enough density that TrueRestore is the obvious default. I'd rather have 140 delighted, adopted locations than 178 half-used seats that churn.

The bottleneck I'd watch isn't sales — it's onboarding capacity. Migrating an owner off Encircle well (data, training, habit) takes real CS hours. The terms
behind this deal.

Restoration + franchise + SaaS jargon, defined — so nothing here is a black box. The panel will know these; this is so I can use them precisely and so the leave-behind is self-contained.

Restoration & insurance

  • Xactimate — Verisk's industry-standard estimating software; the format carriers accept to pay a claim.
  • ESX — the Xactimate estimate file. TrueRestore auto-generates it from field documentation.
  • Approved ticket — the dollar value of a job the carrier actually approves for payment (what the owner collects).
  • Supplement — a follow-up request to the carrier for scope missed or denied the first time. Fewer supplements = the +11.6% mechanism.
  • Mitigation — the emergency drying/water-removal phase; the bulk of AllDry's inbound claim volume.
  • Cycle time — days from job start to a carrier-accepted estimate. Faster = paid faster + crew freed for the next job.
  • Encircle — the incumbent field-documentation tool across much of AllDry; captures, but doesn't build the estimate.

Franchise & commercial

  • FAC — Franchise Advisory Council — elected owners who advise HQ; the body whose endorsement moves the whole network.
  • Preferred / approved vendor — a tool the franchisor officially endorses and steers franchisees toward (they rarely mandate).
  • Multi-unit owner — a franchisee running several territories; one sale = several locations + a peer voice.
  • Royalty — the % of franchisee gross revenue the franchisor collects (~6% at AllDry) — why a ticket lift helps HQ directly.
  • ARR / ACV — Annual Recurring Revenue / Annual Contract Value: the yearly subscription value ($641K full network; ~$11K live today).
  • CAC / LTV — Customer Acquisition Cost / Lifetime Value: low CAC + high LTV is why this is worth owning as a strategic account.
  • Contribution margin — revenue minus variable cost; near-100% on the ticket lift because the work is already performed.
