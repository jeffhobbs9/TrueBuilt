# AllDry Network Win Plan — Talk Track / Speaker Script
### TrueRestore · VP, Strategic Accounts · final round

**What this is:** a start-to-finish spoken script for the ~30-minute presentation, mapped to the 24 slides in `truerestore-alldry-presentation.html` (slides 1–23 present linearly; slide 24 is a **backup** you jump to only if challenged on Encircle). The words below are what to *say* — natural, first-person, out loud. The deck carries the anchors; you carry the story. (Every slide also has these notes built in — press **N** in the deck.)

**How to rehearse:** read it out loud 3×. First time for words, second for timing, third with the deck open advancing as you go. Then throw the deck away and just talk to it — if you know the five section headlines below, you can rebuild the whole thing live.

**The spine (memorize this — it's the whole pitch in one breath):**
> It's a claims business → the estimate that gets paid is the product → Encircle captures but doesn't close that loop → TrueRestore does, and we have live proof in Seattle → so I win the network with peer-proof and franchisor endorsement, not a mandate → and I'd earn the endorsement, not beg it.

## SLIDE 2 — The thesis (~50 sec)
> "Everything I'm about to say rests on one idea. AllDry is a claims business. And in a claims business, the money is won or lost on one thing: how fast and how completely a job becomes a **carrier-accepted estimate**. That's the product — the estimate that actually gets paid.
>
> Here's the gap. Encircle — which most of the network runs — is a good field-documentation tool. But it *captures*; it doesn't close the loop to revenue. The owner still has to bounce to Xactimate to build the estimate that gets paid. TrueRestore closes that loop. And I'm not arguing this in theory — we already have living proof inside their own brand, in Seattle. Hold onto Seattle; it comes back all deck."

*Advance.*

## SLIDE 3 — What TrueRestore is / why the ticket rises (~55 sec)
> "Quick on the product, because the 'why' matters. TrueRestore is field capture built for claims — photos, moisture mapping, contents, time-and-materials, 360° tours. Then AI turns that capture into a carrier-ready Xactimate ESX — no re-keying into a separate estimator.
>
> And here's the mechanism behind that +11.6% approved-ticket number, because it isn't magic: a fully documented job gives the adjuster no reason to deny line items. Complete proof means fewer denials and supplements, so more of the scope the crew *already performed* actually gets approved. Carriers approve what's proven — TrueRestore makes every line provable, and then writes the estimate for you. One more thing: TrueRestore is formerly Capabuild, now in the TrueBuilt family, so estimating is the platform's origin, not a bolt-on."

*Advance.*

## SLIDE 16 — Sales process (~45 sec)
> "The process I'd actually run. A location pipeline, repeated about 175 times: Aware, Qualified, Value-proven, Committed, Adopted, Advocate. Notice the last two. Most reps stop at Committed — signed. In a peer network that's a mistake, because an unused seat doesn't just churn, it becomes *negative* proof — 'we tried it, didn't stick.' ## SLIDE 17 — Qualified vs Committed (~40 sec)

## THE WORKING SESSION (~10 min) — likely curveballs + how you respond

**"The franchisor's gone cold / says no."**
> "Fine — that's my whole contrarian point. I never depended on a top-down yes. I keep going bottoms-up: more vocal owners, regional reference clusters, busy-season urgency. A franchisor rarely *blocks* a tool owners are demanding — and demand is what I'm manufacturing. I revisit endorsement when the groundswell makes it a formality."

**"Encircle cuts their price / matches you."**
> "Price was never my wedge — it's already a wash. Encircle can match $300; what they can't match is closing the loop to the paid estimate. My pitch is the approved ticket and the killed double-entry, not the sticker. If anything, a price war just tells me they're scared of the loop."

**"Seattle churns / goes quiet."**
> "Then I have a much bigger problem than this quarter's number, and I'd treat it as a five-alarm fire — because my whole flywheel starts there. I'd get on a plane, find out if it's product or relationship, and fix it before I scale a single new region. Better to find that out on one account than on fifty."

**"Your 11.6% is one data point."**
> "Agreed — that's exactly why I open the ROI on the *conservative* case, and why one of my first moves is broadening the evidence with San Bernardino, Austin, and the first wave before I ever put a number in front of the advisory council. I'd rather under-claim and over-deliver."

**"You're too slow — we need the network faster."**
> "I can go faster on *signatures*. I can't go faster on *adoption* without manufacturing churn, and in a peer network churn is contagious. I'd rather commit to 40 adopted than 90 signed-and-churning. But tell me the real constraint — if it's a board number, let's talk about where I'd add onboarding capacity to safely pull the curve forward."

**"Why should the franchisor care?"**
> "Because it's their KPI, not mine. Franchisee profitability and consistency *is* the franchisor's job. I'm not asking them to sell software — I'm handing them a lift to their owners' P&L and a story for the conference stage. I make Schlafer the hero."

## GLOSSARY — terms you must own cold
**FAC** = Franchise Advisory Council (elected owners who advise HQ; their endorsement moves the network). · **Xactimate** = the estimating software carriers accept to pay a claim. · **ESX** = the Xactimate estimate file TrueRestore auto-generates. · **Approved ticket** = the dollar amount the carrier actually approves per job (what the owner collects). · **Supplement** = a follow-up ask to the carrier for scope missed/denied first time — fewer of these = the +11.6%. · **Mitigation** = the emergency drying phase (bulk of inbound claim volume). · **Cycle time** = days from job start to a carrier-accepted estimate. · **Encircle** = the incumbent capture tool (documents, but doesn't build the estimate). · **Preferred/approved vendor** = a tool the franchisor endorses and steers owners toward (rarely mandates). · **Royalty** = the % of franchisee gross HQ collects (~6% at AllDry). · **ARR/ACV** = Annual Recurring Revenue / Annual Contract Value ($641K full network; ~$11K live). · **CAC/LTV** = Customer Acquisition Cost / Lifetime Value. · **Contribution margin** = revenue minus variable cost — near-100% on the ticket lift because the work's already done.
