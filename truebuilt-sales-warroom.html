Assignment · Part 2 (5 min)

Building a Pipeline
That Hits Number, Every Quarter

Construction sales cycles are long (60–120+ days) and relationship-dense. A pipeline that only fills from cold outbound will always be feast-or-famine. This is the system, not a single tactic.

Prospecting Methods & Lead Generation

Signal-based targeting

Use ENR's regional Top Contractor / Top Specialty Contractor lists and Dodge/ConstructConnect bid-board activity to find firms that are actively bidding heavy volume right now — active bidders feel the takeoff bottleneck more acutely than idle ones. Track trigger events: new project awards, a new hire into a Director/VP of Preconstruction role (a new leader evaluating tools in their first 90 days is a warm window), M&A activity, or a headcount freeze forcing "bid more with the same team."

Warm channels first

LinkedIn Sales Navigator filtered by title (Chief Estimator, Director/VP Preconstruction, Precon Manager) and company size. Local trade associations — AGC of California, ABC chapters, ENR networking nights, industry conferences like Advancing Preconstruction — put me in the room with people who've already self-identified as caring about this exact problem, which converts far better than cold email.

Referral flywheel

Construction is a small, relationship-driven world — GCs and subs already work together on the same jobsites. Every closed pilot customer gets asked for two warm introductions to a peer GC or a specialty sub they partner with regularly. A referral from someone already using TrueBuilt converts at a completely different rate than cold outbound, and it's nearly free.

Content-led inbound

Short, specific content aimed at estimators — "why a commercial takeoff still eats a day and a half," case studies on the Capabuild/restoration angle — gives the AE something to send instead of a cold pitch, and lets warm leads self-select into a call already half-sold.

Building and Maintaining the Pipeline

  • 3–4x coverage — carry pipeline worth 3–4x quota at every stage. With a realistic 20–25% close rate on a construction-software cycle this long, anything less and a single slipped deal blows the quarter.
  • Multi-thread from meeting one — never rely on a single champion. Build the relationship with the economic buyer (President/COO, or CFO on larger firms) and, where relevant, IT/security in parallel — construction leadership moves slowly and collectively, and a single-threaded deal dies the moment the champion changes jobs or gets busy during bid season.
  • Bid-cycle-aware pilots — anchor the next step to a real, already-scheduled bid on the prospect's calendar ("run TrueBuilt in parallel on your next live bid") instead of an abstract "free trial." It's a lower-friction ask and it produces real, defensible before/after numbers.
  • CRM discipline — every touch logged, every open opportunity tagged to a MEDDPICC stage (see Live Qualifier tab), and every deal carries a dated next step. No deal is allowed to just "go quiet."
  • Weekly self-audit — track new discovery calls booked, qualified opportunities added, and pilots in shadow mode against a weekly target, not just a monthly one — problems caught weekly are cheap to fix; problems caught monthly are not.

Qualifying a TrueBuilt Customer in the First Meeting

Fit signals to confirm

  • Real bid volume — rule of thumb, 3+ full-time estimators or 8+ bids/month, below which the ROI math doesn't clear a meaningful bar
  • Current workflow is manual — Bluebeam/On-Screen Takeoff/Excel with no computer-vision assist, and estimator bandwidth is visibly capping how many jobs they can chase
  • A named budget owner and a realistic decision path (precon director vs. CFO vs. IT security review)
  • A timing trigger — busy season approaching, recent losses on bid-day timing, or a headcount freeze

Disqualifiers to listen for

  • Locked into a multi-year enterprise agreement (Procore, Sage, Autodesk Construction Cloud) with no near-term renewal window
  • Bid volume too small to justify a platform switch — a one- or two-estimator shop is a better fit for a lighter, cheaper point tool
  • No one owns preconstruction as a function — buying decision would need to be invented from scratch, signaling a much longer cycle

"We already have Bluebeam / On-Screen Takeoff — why add another tool?"+

"That's the right instinct — an estimator who blindly trusts a number is a bigger risk than one who doesn't. Human-in-the-loop review is a core design decision, not an afterthought. Let the most skeptical person on the team be the one who validates it.

Speak Their Language

Every Term
Explained Simply

You don't need to fake construction fluency — you need to not get lost when Adam uses it. These are the terms that come up in every precon conversation.

Takeoff
a.k.a. Quantity Takeoff (QTO)
Measuring and counting everything shown on a set of drawings — doors, duct runs, square footage, rebar — so it can be priced. It's the first, most time-consuming step of every bid.
"A takeoff that eats a day and a half by hand can drop to a few hours with computer-vision assist."
Bid Leveling
Comparing incoming subcontractor bids for the same scope side by side, so you're comparing apples to apples on price and what's actually included, not just the bottom-line number.
Value Engineering (VE)
Proposing alternate materials, systems, or methods that hit the same design intent for less cost — usually surfaced during precon when a budget is over target.
ITB — Invitation to Bid
The formal request a GC or owner sends out asking contractors/subs to submit a price for a scope of work, usually with a hard deadline.
Schedule of Values (SOV)
A line-item breakdown of the total contract price by task or trade — used for billing/payment applications as the job progresses.
Bid-Hit Ratio (Win Rate)
Bids won divided by bids submitted. Industry average sits around 20–25%; top performers with strong repeat-client relationships hit 30–50%.
Chief Estimator
The senior person who owns pricing accuracy and estimating methodology across all of a firm's bids — often the institutional-knowledge risk if they leave.
GMP — Guaranteed Maximum Price
A contract structure where the GC guarantees a project won't exceed an agreed cost ceiling — puts extra pressure on estimating accuracy since overruns come out of the GC's own margin.
Buyout
Finalizing subcontracts after winning the bid — the moment a missed takeoff quantity usually surfaces, since actual sub pricing reveals what got missed in the original estimate.
Design-Bid-Build vs. Design-Build
Design-Bid-Build: design is finished, then GCs competitively bid the completed set. Design-Build: one entity handles design and construction together, often with GCs involved earlier and pricing evolving alongside design.
Annual Construction Volume (ACV)
The total dollar value of work a firm manages in a year — some software (like Procore) prices off this instead of per-seat.
Computer Vision Takeoff
Software that automatically recognizes plan elements (walls, doors, fixtures) directly from a PDF drawing and extracts quantities, instead of an estimator manually clicking/measuring each one. This is TrueBuilt's core differentiator.
